# Elias Grave — Story Bible

Game title: Elias Grave (the hero's name, in the tradition of Max Payne and John Wick)

"Club Noir" is only the nightclub in the tutorial level. It is not the game title.

## Tone Rules (Max Payne + John Wick)

- **The hero is grieving, quiet and relentless.** He speaks rarely: one to five words. His inner voice-over speaks in the cutscene captions. It is bitter, poetic and hard-boiled, and it uses rain and music metaphors.
- **Graphic-novel storytelling.** Every cutscene is a comic-strip set of still panels. High contrast, deep black shadows, rain streaks, neon colour accents (red, cyan, amber) on desaturated scenes. Caption boxes are yellowed paper for voice-over and white balloons for dialogue.
- **The Sandman.** The underworld's folklore name for Elias, like Baba Yaga in John Wick: "the one who puts you to sleep, and you never wake." Goons whisper it. Bosses say it with fear or false bravado. The Choir's villains keep the music theme; the hero's legend plays on sleep.
- **A hidden underworld with its own rules.** The Choir has laws: debts pass to whoever breaks the debtor; a tuning fork is a death notice; no one touches a member's family without the Conductor's word; gold "Choir coins" pay for safe houses and silence. Breaking these rules has a price.
- **Gun-fu and slow motion.** Each level has at least one slow-motion set piece (a dive through glass, a roof jump, a gun duel). Boss finishers end in a slow-motion freeze frame.
- **Grief as the engine.** Elena (his dead wife) and Bell (his dead mentor) haunt the voice-over. The dog Nero is the last living thing he loves.

## 1. Logline and Trigger

**Logline.** A silent ex-killer got his dog back from a mob boss. Now he learns that the boss was only one voice in a choir that owns the city. He must silence every voice, from the docks to the tallest tower in Valmora.

**The hero.** His name is **Elias Grave**. The underworld calls him **"the Sandman"**: the one who puts you to sleep, and you never wake. Bell calls him "Elias" or "boy". Bosses call him "the Sandman" or "Grave". He was once the Choir's top enforcer. He quit when his wife **Elena** died. Elena left him her dog, **Nero**, a tan-and-black shepherd.

**Why this trigger works.** Club Noir was the Choir's money laundry. By the underworld's rule, the man who breaks a debtor takes on his debt. When the hero broke Malaspina, he inherited 4,000,000, and the Choir collected with fire. It grows directly out of the tutorial. It is personal because they killed the man who raised him. It is believable because the Choir always collects.

## 2. The Antagonists: The Choir

The Choir is the secret organisation that runs Valmora. Its public face is **Meridian Holdings**. Its calling card is a silver tuning fork. Its ranks use musical names.

| Rank | Who | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Chorus** | Street crews | Muscle, collections |
| **Tenors** | Crew bosses (Malaspina, Marchetti, Switch) | Run streets, docks, tunnels |
| **Baritones** | Organised crime (casino, smuggling, medicine) | Move money, goods, bodies |
| **Basses ("the Board")** | Commissioner, mayor, judges, bankers | Own the city |
| **First Violin** | The Conductor's personal blade | Enforcer above all ranks |
| **The Conductor ("Maestro")** | Unknown | Head of everything |

**The Choir's rules:**
1. Debts pass to whoever breaks the debtor.
2. The tuning fork is a final notice.
3. Gold Choir coins buy safe passage, silence and medical care, no questions asked.
4. No one touches a member's family without the Conductor's word.

The Board broke rule 4 when Malaspina took Nero.

**Hidden backstory (revealed slowly).** The Board planned a coup called **"Silent Night"**: kill the Conductor and sell Valmora to outside money. Malaspina took Nero as the first move. The dog was leverage to force the Sandman to kill his old master. The Conductor found out, faked his death and is now using the Sandman to cut down every traitor.

### The 8 Bosses

1. **Salvatore "Sal the Scale" Marchetti**, Tenor. A huge man in a rubber apron and boots, with a gold fish-scale necklace. He uses a cleaver and a meat hook on a chain (pull, then chop). **Gimmick:** an overhead conveyor swings frozen tuna carcasses across the arena, and crushed-ice patches make the floor slide. He pulls a freezer lever to fill the room with cold fog and hide in it.
2. **Ilsa "Switch" Varga**, Tenor. A wiry punk in a red rail coat and goggles. She fights with a chain whip and rail spikes. **Gimmick:** live trains pass through the arena; headlights and a horn give the warning. A sparking third rail runs along the floor. She throws switch levers to change which track the next train uses. In phase 2 she rides a handcar through the arena.
3. **Count Teodor Brask**, Baritone, owner of the Grand Casino Orfeo. Tall, with a white tuxedo and a monocle. He throws razor-edged playing cards and uses a cane. **Gimmick (glass):** a hall of mirrors. Mirror copies of Brask attack, and the player smashes mirrors to find the real one. Then the fight moves to a giant spinning roulette-wheel floor.
4. **Nerissa Kalt**, Baritone, smuggling queen. She wears a wetsuit under a long oilskin coat and fights with a harpoon gun and a diving knife. **Gimmick (glass):** a glass tunnel under the shark tank. Her missed harpoons crack the glass. Cracks spread, leaks become slippery puddles, and the tunnel floods in stages. The player must climb to the catwalks above the open tank for phase 2.
5. **Dr. Anselm Krieg**, Baritone, the Choir's surgeon. Gaunt, with a surgical gown over a three-piece suit. He uses a bone saw, sedative darts that slow the hero, and defibrillator paddles with an area shock. **Gimmick (glass):** glass isolation wards and a flickering power failure. He attacks in the dark, and the player sees him only in lightning flashes and through glass walls. Hospital beds on wheels act as moving cover.
6. **Commissioner Augusta Vane**, Bass, the police chief. She wears a dress uniform and medals and fights with a riot shield and a shock baton. **Gimmick (glass):** a Victorian palm house. Her snipers outside put laser dots through the glass. Missed shots shatter panes, and glass rains down. She calls in waves of riot police with whistle blasts.
7. **Viktor Lenz, "First Violin"**, the Conductor's blade and the man who replaced the Sandman. Pale, in a white suit, he carries a violin case with a thin sabre inside. He is a mirror of the hero. **Gimmick:** he attacks on a strict beat, telegraphed by a metronome tick, so the player can learn his rhythm. The fight is on the roof of a speeding train, with tunnels to duck under, signal gantries, and cars that decouple.
8. **Augustin Bell, "the Conductor"**, the ultimate boss. He is an old man in shirtsleeves and a waistcoat, with a jeweller's loupe and a cherry-wood pipe. He fights with a weighted pocket-watch chain flail and a sword-cane. He is fast, exact and unafraid. **Gimmick:** the clockwork inside the Spire's crown. Turning gears are moving platforms. A huge pendulum sweeps the arena. Each quarter-hour bell chime sends out a shockwave, which is a callback to Malaspina's ground slam. Phase 3 is on the outside of the giant glass clock face, on the minute hand, in a storm.

## 3. The 8 Levels

### Level 1: "Pier 9"

- **Setting:** The docks and the Pier 9 fish market. The docks are outdoors: container stacks, cranes and the quay edge. The fish auction hall and a walk-in freezer are indoors.
- **Time / weather:** Dawn, sleet, fog horns.
- **Beat:** He wants the collector named on the ledger page. He learns that the fire was not personal to Marchetti; it was "just business," ordered from higher up.
- **Faction:** **The Saltmen**: dock brawlers, pipe men with fish knives, and gaffs (reskinned machetes).
- **Slow-motion set piece:** a shotgun slide across the iced auction floor between rows of tuna.
- **Hook:** cash crates stamped with old Line 0 metro tokens.

- **Slow-motion set piece:** a dive through an isolation ward's glass wall, firing at three Orderlies.

### Level 7: "Last Train from Valmora"

- **Glass:** The glass dome observation car, where the Board's members (the mayor, Judge Orsolya Kern and the banker Cornelius Aldane) cower. It shatters when the fight goes in and out of it.
- **Time / weather:** Midnight. Gale and lightning over the dam.
- **Beat:** He wants the ledgers and the Maestro's name. The cowardly Board gives up without a fight. He learns that the traitors were real, but someone used him to remove them.
- **Faction:** **The Ushers**: grey-suited private guards. They carry pistols and machine guns, so this is the biggest gunfight level.
- **Slow-motion set piece:** a jump between two decoupling cars over the dam viaduct, firing the shotgun in mid-air.
- **Hook / main twist:** Lenz's last words, a perfect copy of Bell's workshop in the last car, and Bell's voice on a gramophone.

### Level 8: "The Spire"

- **Setting:** The Meridian Spire, the tallest tower in Valmora, on its opening night. It is a vertical climb:
  - a marble mirrored lobby fight
  - a **glass elevator** fight as the car rises up the outside of the tower
  - a sky-garden
  - a penthouse with a **glass floor** over a 90-floor drop
  - the clockwork crown with its giant **glass clock face**
- **Time / weather:** Midnight, at the peak of the storm.
- **Beat:** He wants the truth and an end to it. He learns everything (see §4).
- **Faction:** **The Choir Proper**: elite men in black tie with every weapon type.
- **Boss:** Augustin Bell, the Conductor, in three phases: the gear room, the pendulum hall, and the outside clock face and minute hand.
- **Slow-motion set piece:** the glass-floor penthouse cracks under a machine-gun volley. The hero dives across the falling shards to the stair rail.
- **Dog moment:** in phase 3, Nero bursts in and bites Bell's flail arm. This makes a one-time opening for a finisher.

## 4. The Twist

**Augustin Bell is alive and is the Conductor.** The body in the fire was a Choir accountant wearing Bell's ring. Bell learned of "Silent Night." Malaspina's kidnapping of Nero was the Board's opening move: leverage to force the Sandman to kill Bell. Bell faked his own death to aim his old student at every traitor. Hale delivered every lead on Bell's orders.

**Where it lands:** At the end of Level 7 (the gramophone), with the confrontation in Level 8.

**Setups, in order:**
- **Prologue:** Bell's line about someone who "keeps time." Nero ignores the body.
- **Every level:** Each boss lair has a Bell & Sons clock stopped at 3:17. Designers must place one in every boss arena.
- **L3:** Brask's photo with one face cut out.
- **L4:** Kalt: "Somebody always understands the nod."
- **L5:** Krieg: "Your Maestro is closer than you think."
- **L6:** Nero reacts to the cherry tobacco. Hale's phone call.
- **L7:** Lenz's "left guard" line.

**A smaller twist at L6:** The trusted informant, Hale, was working for the Maestro all along.

## 5. Recurring Threads

**Nero's role:**
- He saves the hero in the prologue.
- He is a scout in L2 (barks at hidden paths).
- He guards Mags in L5.
- He senses Bell in L6.
- He refuses to leave the hero in L7.
- He makes the decisive assist in L8.

Designers can add an optional "Nero, hold!" assist in the levels where Nero is present: he bites and pins one goon.

**Side characters:**
- **Margaux "Mags" Ferrand:** the Club Noir bartender who gave Nero back. She hides the hero at her shut-down jazz bar, **The Low Note**, the hub between levels. She drives him in L3–L4, is shot, and survives in L5. She is the honest heart of the story.
- **Inspector Ottavio Hale:** a tired, rain-coated detective. He gives leads by phone booth; every level ends with a payphone ringing. He is secretly the Maestro's go-between and dies in L6.
- **Elena:** the hero's dead wife. She appears only in voice-over and in brief sepia memory panels: her hand on Nero's head, her chessboard, her red scarf.
- **The residents:** As in the tutorial, they switch off their lights when fights start outside their windows.

**Visual motifs:**
- the silver tuning fork as the Choir's calling card
- clocks stopped at 3:17
- glass that breaks more as the city's lies crack
- rain in every level until the ending
- the colour red for the Choir: red carpet, Switch's coat, the gala wax seal
- cherry pipe smoke
- Elena's red scarf, which the hero keeps in his inside pocket

**Cutscene style:** comic panels with yellowed voice-over captions, white dialogue balloons, a red tint on any Choir symbol, and a sepia tint for memories.

**Hierarchy line in each boss's last words:** Each beaten boss names the next rank up, so the player always knows where the story goes next.

## 6. The Ending

Bell hangs from the minute hand, outside the shattered clock face. He offers the hero the city: *"The Choir needs a Conductor. Come home, Elias."*

The hero looks at Nero, then at Bell, and says: **"I am home."**

The minute hand reaches twelve. The midnight bells ring, and the shockwave shakes Bell loose. He falls into the storm. The hero does not push him; time does.
